# Auto Scouting — automatic RAMP ROI placement

When a video is loaded on `/video-scouting/auto`, the two counting boxes are placed over
the RED and BLUE RAMPS automatically, from any camera angle. The counting logic (the
single scoring line inside each box, `GateMultiTracker`) is **unchanged** — only where the
boxes go, and how they get there, is new.

Code: `lib/scouting/placement/` (library, unit-tested) + `app/video-scouting/auto/`
(`RoiPlacementPanel.tsx`, `PlacementOverlay.tsx`, drag/resize in `AutoVideoPlayer.tsx`,
orchestration in `page.tsx`). OpenCV.js (`@techstark/opencv-js`) is loaded on demand from
`/api/opencv`.

## Field dimensions: where every number comes from

`lib/scouting/placement/field_model.json` is generated by `scripts/build-field-model.mjs`,
which holds the raw measurements with a comment on each; the JSON carries a `source` string
next to every value.

| Value | Source |
|---|---|
| AprilTag family 36h11, 8.125 in overall | Competition Manual (CM) §9.10 p.74 |
| Tag black square 6.5 in, centred on the GOAL face, centre 9.25 in below the top edge | CM Fig 9-19 p.75 |
| Top edge of the GOAL front panel 38.75 in | CM §9.7 / Fig 9-9; Field Acceptance Checklist (FAC) 1.8 |
| Tag IDs: red GOAL 24, blue GOAL 20; OBELISK 21-23 excluded (not deterministic) | CM §9.10, §9.6 |
| Red ALLIANCE AREA on the audience's left; goal corners | CM §9.5, Fig 9-18 |
| ARTIFACT diameter 5 in (ROI clearance) | CM §9.9 |
| 36 tiles, 6×6 | CM §9.2, Fig 9-5 |
| GOAL backboard 15 in above the open top (53.75 in) | CM §9.7 / Fig 9-9 (matches the CAD, 1365.2 mm) |
| Tile pitch 23.563 in, perimeter walls, GOAL position/angle, tag-face plane, RAMP rails, tape centre-lines, outlines of every ALLIANCE-coloured panel (GOAL front / rear / backboard / archway panels, lower-RAMP blockers) | Official field CAD (below) |

**The manual does not state the GOAL's x/y placement, the GOAL face angle, or any RAMP
dimension.** CM §9.1 names the official 3D CAD model as *"the official representation of the
DECODE FIELD"* (±1 in), so those values were measured from it: *DECODE presented by RTX Full
Field STEP.20250905 - V1* (`am-5700_Full.step`), from the Playing Field Resources page
(`ftc-resources.firstinspires.org/ftc/archive/2026/field`). The STEP file was loaded with
OpenCascade (`occt-import-js`) and each named part was measured: perimeter rails, tiles,
`Goal Front/Rear/Backboard/Archway` panels, `Lower Ramp Blocker (Small Section)`,
`Red/Blue Goal AprilTag Sticker`, the upper
(`Ramp 28in Two-Hole Extrusion`) and lower (`Ramp 45in Two-Hole Extrusion`) ramp rails, and
every `Taped Field` tape part. The tape positions were cross-checked against the FAC
(section 3: e.g. the back LAUNCH LINE apex over X3, the front apex over X1). To regenerate the
JSON, edit the constants in the build script and run `node scripts/build-field-model.mjs`.
The STEP file itself is not in the repo.

Coordinate frame (documented at the top of the JSON): inches, origin at the field centre,
z up, **+x toward the red ALLIANCE AREA**, and +y toward the audience. Note that this puts the
RED GOAL/RAMP at *negative* x.

The ROI region for each ramp runs from the SQUARE end of the upper RAMP (inside the GOAL)
to the GATE end of the lower RAMP, plus one ARTIFACT of clearance. The scoring line sits
6 in down the lower RAMP from where ARTIFACTS land on it. As a result, every ARTIFACT is seen
above the line before it crosses it, which the unchanged crossing counter requires. The
direction is set from the projected goal→gate motion.

## Pipeline

Every method produces 2D↔3D correspondences, then goes through the same steps:
`solvePose` (solvePnP, or solvePnPRansac for more than 4 points; the FOV is refined when
there are at least 6 points), then `projectRamp` (cv.projectPoints), then the sanity checks.
The checks are: the ramp is in front of the camera, its projection is convex, at least 60% of
it is in the frame, its area is plausible, and the camera is above the floor. The result is a
normalized quad plus the axis-aligned counting zone, the line and the direction.

- **A — AprilTag anchor**: GOAL tags 20 and 24 are detected on the full frame, then on
  upscaled crops if none are found. Each tag must be at least 20 px per side, and the
  reprojection error must be at most 3 px.
  - **Corner order** (fixed 2026-10-04): OpenCV returns a GOAL tag's corners rotated 180°
    from the model's old order. Measured on 12 videos: 0.4–4 px with the fix, 18–66 px
    without, so method A had never worked on real video.
  - **Always fused with the floor** (2026-10-04). The tag corners pin the camera's position
    but not its field of view. The GOAL tags sit at one end of the field at nearly the same
    depth, so the focal length trades off against distance.
    - Measured on 7 real videos with 2–3 tags in view: the tag-only fit stayed within 0.1 px
      of its best over 12–16° of FOV (Hawaii, Bedford, Lake Orion), and over the whole
      30–110° range on DC1 and Marshall (`fovCurve.ts`).
    - On Hawaii 720p the tags alone chose 41° where the floor says 47.5°. That put the blue
      lane 1.5 ARTIFACTS onto the GOAL panel, and blue counted 2 against 47 (now 42).
    - So the tag corners are always fitted **jointly** with the floor features from B (the
      tags get as much weight as all the tape points together). The fusion is used when it
      also reprojects the tag corners within 6 px and passes the acceptance test.
    - When it cannot be used, the frame keeps the tag-only pose but also runs B. B's
      placement wins when it explains the frame (`frameEvidence ≥ weakEvidence`). A lock on
      tag-only frames says so: "field of view from the GOAL tags alone".
  - A pose from tags alone must also agree with the GOAL colours and the field floor (the
    acceptance test below, without the tape). In the Bedford phone video a 104° pose fitted
    both tags to 0.8 px while missing the field. A pose that fails is left to B.
- **Lens distortion** (`pose.withLensDistortion`, 2026-10-04): final poses (the joint tag +
  floor fit and B's ICP result) also try a radial distortion k1 with the FOV. A coarse grid
  is followed by a finer one, fitted on every point the pinhole pose roughly explains. It is
  kept only if it lowers their mean error by 20% and a full robust solve with it explains at
  least as many points, more closely.
  - The synthetic tests check that a k1 = −0.20 lens is recovered (−0.205) with the RAMPS
    in place, and that a pinhole camera gets none.
  - On the 17 real videos no lens was needed. On DC1 about 500–680 floor and tag points fit
    the pinhole to 1.8 px, and the best k1 improves that by under 2%. The intrinsics carry
    k1 everywhere: projection, saved placements, the counter's lanes.
- **B — field-plane anchor** (`markings.ts`, `fieldPlane.ts`):
  - *Whole-model alignment* (the main path; works at any angle, down to 240p). The whole CAD
    model is projected with a candidate camera and compared with what the image shows:
    - **Floor**: the TILE region is found by colour, not by assuming the frame centre is
      floor. The candidates are the three largest regions of each of the commonest smooth,
      low-chroma colours anywhere in the frame. This copes with grey or warm-lit (beige)
      tiles, and with close-ups where a GOAL fills the centre.
      - **GOAL support**: the GOALS stand on the TILES, so the regions found just below
        the bases of upright red / blue objects (GOALS with their RAMPS) rank first. Among
        those, the one with the most white tape wins. Without this, an atrium's stone floor
        (Maxwell) out-scored the field on tape-like slab joints. Flat red / blue blobs are
        ignored, because a saturated patch of a blue venue floor (Bedford) lies on that floor
        rather than standing on the field.
      - **Retry**: if the alignment fails, it is tried once more on the next distinct
        candidate, so the field model decides. In Bedford the largest region of the tile
        colour was the gym wall. The floor colour of the first well-explained frame is then
        tried first on later frames.
      - **Scoring**: the score is asymmetric. Floor seen outside the projected field counts
        against a pose, but parts of the field hidden by GOALS, robots or the near wall do
        not.
    - **ALLIANCE-coloured structures**: each GOAL (front / rear / backboard / archway
      panels) and its lower-RAMP blocker, from the CAD, is compared with the red or blue
      region as an overlap (IoU). The overlap is counted in a window around the predicted
      silhouette, so spectators' shirts or banners elsewhere do not matter. It also
      includes the nearer GOAL hiding the farther one. The GOALS are tall, so they fix
      camera height and FOV, and they break the field's symmetry. A smooth distance term
      keeps pulling a GOAL toward its colour when it misses entirely.
    - **Tape**: capped chamfer distance both ways between the projected tape centre-lines
      and the white, red and blue tape pixels (red and blue measured relative to the floor
      colour).
    - **Search**: a coarse grid of cameras (all azimuths, 8–78° elevation, 70–540 in,
      35–80° FOV), pre-filtered with a cheap score. On top of the grid come close-range
      views aimed at each GOAL, and views *anchored on each GOAL's colour blob*: for every
      viewing direction, the distance makes the silhouette match the blob's area and the
      aim puts it on the blob. The 60 most different candidates get a quick local fit, the
      best 5 are refined by Nelder–Mead (pose + FOV), and the best 2 are polished by tape
      ICP.
    - **Acceptance** (`evaluatePose`, applied to every B pose): every GOAL in view must
      overlap its colour by ≥ 30%. At least 70% of the detected floor must lie inside the
      projected field, and at least 8% of the field must be seen as floor. When ≥ 40 tape
      samples are on the floor, ≥ 20% must land on tape. All thresholds are shares, so
      they mean the same at every resolution.
    - **Reprojection-error limits** (3 px for A, 4 px for B, 2 px for "low confidence")
      are pixels of a frame 640 px wide, the width the alignment works at. A 1280×720
      frame's error is halved before it is compared: the same misfit (field build
      tolerance, lens distortion, 1 in tape) covers twice the pixels there. Before this, a
      720p video failed on a 4.15 px fit that was 2.1 px at the alignment's own scale.
  - *TILE grid* (fallback): vanishing-point line families are matched to the seam grid,
    with ±1-tile shifts and 90° rotations tried as hypotheses, and the GOAL colours pick
    the orientation. Its result must pass the same acceptance test.

  Colour masks: GOAL pixels need HSV saturation ≥ 140. Measured on both real videos, 90%
  of GOAL panel pixels have S ≥ 150, while carpets and drapes are duller. Broadcast score
  banners are removed as straight-sided bands at the frame's top or bottom edge. The
  alignment runs on a copy at most 640 px wide.
- **B2 — hook only**: `registerKeypointModel()` in `placer.ts` accepts a future trained
  keypoint model that returns correspondences.
- **C — 4 taps**: the corners in model order (red-goal corner first, then clockwise from
  above), with a field diagram and Undo.
- **D — manual**: drag a box, drag its corner handle to resize, or use the sliders. This
  overrides the automatic result for the session.

**Stability**: placement samples the video every 0.2 s and stops at 30 usable frames.
- **Frames without a field** (intro cards, announcer shots, replays) are handled two ways.
  A 240 px pre-check rejects most in about 5 ms and skips 0.75 s. When a full search still
  fails on a frame, sampling jumps 1 s ahead. Frames that look like the same shot (a 32×18
  thumbnail within 6 grey levels) are skipped for the next 2 s of video, and each time the
  same shot fails again that wait doubles, up to 16 s. A results graphic shown for 20 s
  (Maxwell) then costs 4 searches instead of 11, so it cannot use up the time budget.
- **Tracking**: the first 3 B frames are solved from scratch. Later frames are tracked
  (about 0.4–0.5 s each) from the pose that most of those agree on, so a single wrong solve
  cannot steer every later frame.
- **Lock**: the ROI corners are the median over the largest group of mutually consistent
  frames, so a broadcast camera cut cannot mix two shots.

Placement never runs while counting.

A camera-moved monitor tracks up to 80 features from the lock frame using Lucas–Kanade flow.
It triggers only when most features shift together (a homography with ≥50% inliers moving
the frame corners by more than 2%), or when most features are lost (a cut). A robot covering a
few features does not trigger it. When it triggers, counting pauses and placement re-runs.
**This applies only to the legacy scoring-line counter** (boxes without a camera pose). The
RAMP queue counter follows the camera itself (`viewTracker.ts`). Running the monitor during
RAMP counting made live mode freeze about 10 s into an ordinary match: the monitor saw most of
the lock frame's features change as robots and people moved, re-ran placement and wiped the
count.

**Search budget** (for videos that open on something other than the field):
- While no frame has placed, the search continues for up to 180 s of wall-clock time
  (`maxSearchSeconds`).
- After failures in a row, the sampling step doubles from 1 s up to 8 s (`maxFailStepSec`), so
  25 s of announcers is passed with a few searches.
- The 60 s budget (`maxSeconds`) only applies once 5 frames of one view have placed.
- A placed frame that disagrees with the last one is a new view (the phone zoomed out or turned
  to the field). It gets at least 45 s of its own (`firstPlacedBudgetSec`).
- When the one-tag floor fit has already searched the floor and failed, method B is not run
  again on the same frame.
- Neither budget ends the search before 20 frames have been looked at
  (`minAttemptsBeforeTimeout`). On a busy or slow computer a 1080p frame can take 30 s. Brisbane
  North failed that way after 6 frames in 180 s (2026-10-06, the computer was training models).
  Run alone it places from 32 frames. N2 (Worlds stands) and the Thailand stream also failed
  only under load.

Brisbane North Playoff M4 (1080p) opens on about 25 s of announcers. It used to run out of time
before the field appeared. It now places from t = 0 (method B). `placement.test.ts` checks the
budget with 40 s of non-field footage and no wall-clock budget before the first placed frame.

**Persistence**: the confirmed ROIs, pose, FOV and method are saved in `localStorage`, keyed
by file name, size and last-modified time. Reopening the same video restores them in about
0.3 s. Every run is logged with its method and reprojection error: in the debug log, the
console, and `autoScout.roiLog.v1`, which is included in *Export debug log*.

**Maxwell's results graphic (2026-10-03).** In Chrome, Maxwell (720p) locked on its 22 s
results graphic: FOV 44°, 30/31 frames, low confidence. The counter then never found the field
and counted 0.
- **Why.** That graphic's red and blue score panels pass for the GOALS at `frameEvidence`
  0.23–0.27, depending on the frame. Node's samples fell under 0.25 and its search moved on to
  the field. The browser sampled frames just above 0.25, treated the graphic as a
  well-explained view, and filled all 30 frames from it.
- **The cutoff.** Measured with the app's colour decoding (BT.709), every 20 s through all 17
  videos:
  - real field lock frames: 0.48–1.37;
  - placed real frames between 0.25 and 0.4: only 5 of 129, and every video has most frames
    above 0.4;
  - correct synthetic placements: 0.51 and up.
- **The fix.** `weakEvidence` is 0.4, so such a graphic is treated as weak (re-searched less,
  outweighed by field frames).
- **Checked on seven videos** (Maxwell, Atlanta, South East, Hawaii 720p, Bedford 720p and
  1080p, Lake Orion Q10), current and new cutoff side by side:
  - identical on five;
  - Bedford 1080p within 0.1% of the frame;
  - Maxwell (field at 26.5 s either way) within 0.7%.
- **Rejected alternative: a stricter floor-tape test.** The graphic matches only 27–32% of the
  tape, against ≥ 49% for real frames. But correct synthetic placements go as low as 46%, so
  the margin is too thin.
- **In Chrome with the new cutoff**, Maxwell locked on the field (24.15 s, 2.42 px). The
  counter calibrated from the staged ARTIFACTS and counted red 25 / blue 2 live (official
  26 / 0).
- **Only well-explained frames end the search.** The 30-frame target now counts only frames at
  or above `weakEvidence` (method A frames always count). Weak frames still vote in the lock,
  weighted by their evidence. Re-placing all 17 videos in Node with this change:
  - 12 are identical;
  - Hartland moves by 0.1% of the frame;
  - North East and Bedford 1080p stop at 18 and 28 strong frames, with identical boxes;
  - Brisbane 1080p locks at 30.6 s instead of 38.2 s, and its count moves by one.
- **Still open: Maxwell is flagged low confidence in Chrome.** It still locks on only 7 field
  frames (fewer than 10 is flagged), although the pose is right. What stops it is the 45 s a
  new view gets (`firstPlacedBudgetSec`), not the frame target. Within that time the field

### Any camera angle (synthetic, randomized)

Renders use random cameras all around the field at 426×240 to 1280×720, in four kinds: high
stands, overhead, low side, and phone at the field wall. The field must fill ≥ 12% of the
frame. Each render includes real-footage nuisances:
- a tinted floor;
- a textured crowd with red and blue shirts;
- a coloured carpet;
- robots, and a referee in front;
- a broadcast score banner;
- blur and noise;
- AprilTags on only half the renders, TILE seams on 40%.

Each render is a single frame, placed by the same per-frame path as the app.

Two independent sweeps of 40 views each (different random seeds), single frame per view:

| Camera kind | Within 3% | Wrong box (> 5%) | Nothing placed (safe) |
|---|---|---|---|
| High stands | 19 / 20 | 0 | 1 |
| Overhead | 17 / 20 | 0 (+1 with only one of two ramps) | 2 |
| Low side | 15 / 20 | 2 | 3 |
| Phone at the field wall | 16 / 20 | 2 | 2 |
| **All** | **67 / 80** | **4** | **8** |

By resolution, 426×240 is the hardest (14 / 18); 1280×720 was 16 / 19. About 3–7 s per cold frame at 640 px
(Node; the browser is similar).

**Re-run on 2026-10-03 with the current code:** 66 / 80 within 3%, **7 wrong**, 7 nothing placed
(seed 7: 33 / 4 / 3; seed 11: 33 / 3 / 4). Two views got worse since the table above, both
from the 720p-round changes to method B, which were checked on the real videos but not on this
sweep at the time:
- **Low side #38 (seed 11): safe failure → wrong box (6–7%).** The cause is the retry on the
  next floor candidate: without it the view still fails safely.
- **Phone at the wall #35 (seed 7): within 1.4% → 10.5%.** This one is wrong even without the
  retry, so the cause is the new ranking of floor candidates by GOAL support.

Both changes are what placed Maxwell and Bedford correctly. In a real video, the 30-frame lock
with its consistency grouping is the guard against a single wrong frame. The wrong synthetic
poses still match the floor tape well (76–96%), so a stricter acceptance test would not
separate them.

The failures are safe: nothing is placed and the 4-tap fallback is offered. They happen when
a GOAL is completely hidden by a person standing in front, or on an extreme low view with
almost no visible floor. The remaining wrong answers are very low (≈ 20 in) or side-on views
with the near GOAL filling a large part of the frame. The 3-frame majority vote and the
consistency lock guard against those in a real video.

`npm test` covers:
- four fixed views with tags;
- six realistic views without tags or seams, from any angle, each within 3%;
- the field-model checks;
- the acceptance test rejecting the field turned around.

## Known limitations

- **Counting**: see [`auto-ramp-counting.md`](auto-ramp-counting.md).

- **UI blocking**: a cold frame (the first frame, plus 2 majority-vote frames) blocks the
  page for about 3 s each, because placement runs on the main thread. Moving it to a Web
  Worker would fix this.
- **Extreme views**: a camera near floor level (≈ 20 in), or side-on with the near GOAL
  covering much of the frame, can still be placed wrongly or not at all. Tap the corners
  (method C) or drag the boxes (method D) in that case.
- **Far-away shots**: when the field is tiny in the frame (under about 12% of it), the floor
  region is too small to find. The ramps would be only a few pixels wide, too small to count
  anyway.
- **One tag with nothing else**: a single tag and no visible floor features is placed with
  low confidence. Four coplanar points and an assumed 70° FOV give only an approximate ramp
  far from the tag.
